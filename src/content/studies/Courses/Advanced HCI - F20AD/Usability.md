Usability is the measure of the quality of the interaction with a service. A crucial part of this is being accessible, which means to remove barriers of usage between certain demographics. According to an ISO standard, it mainly includes effectiveness (can the user carry out what they wish to), efficiency (how effective the system is, speed wise, smoothness), and satisfaction (how the user feels about using).

dark patterns are when users are deceived or manipulated into taking actions that may or may not be good for them.

Disabilities are mismatches between the needs of the individual and the environment/service.

Deprivation of basic senses account for a majority of these, and are called physical exclusions. 
Conceptual exclusion is when users can't process or comprehend what information is given to them.
Economic exclusion is when users are unable to afford the essential tech for some service, exacerbating the digital divide.
Cognition exclusion includes perception and learning disabilities
Cultural exclusion is when inappropriate assumptions exclude people of certain groups, preferring other ones.

Accessibiltiy is ensuring none of this exclusion takes place, by ensuring the design accomodates all kinds of users. 

Its importance has even led it to become a legal issue, where it is unlawful to discriminate or not accomodate users.

One way is to ensure ur work is compatible with assistive tech like screen readers and eye tracking

universal design principles
- equitable use: be equally welcoming and valuable for everyone
- flexibility use: provide plenty of choices for how the task can be accomplishes
- simple and intuitive use: be intuitive immediately and not require too many instructions to navigate
- perceptible info: be clear what the purpose of the application is.
- tolerance for error: aim to prevent user errors and provide assistance for when there is an error
- low physical effort: should not be difficult to use or require the user to put in lots of effort
- size and space for approach: should be the appropriate size for the user and their agent 

security in HCI:
- confidentiality: prevent unauthorised disclosure or access to infromation
- integrity: information should not be tampered with
- availability: ensure information is accessible in a timely manner

Must have trade-offs between security, usability, and full functionality. The following guidelines are good ideas ensuring a good balance of "usable security":
- provide paths of least resistance, only requiring security interactions when needed
- minimise user interaction for efficient minimum security
- allow users to review security choices and revoke mistakes
- indicate consequence(s) of taking an action
- the easiest and most effective method to perform a task must have the highest level of security
