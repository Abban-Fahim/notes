Popularly known in simple terms as "neurons that fire together, wire together". The entire postulate states that two neurons that exhibit activity at similar times will tend to reinforce their relatedness through synaptic means. The change of synaptic weights is primarily focused on locality and joint activity, and hence we can model this system with the dynamic system involving pre-synaptic neuron $j$ and post-synaptic neuron $i$

$$\frac{dw_{ij}}{dt}=F(w_{ij};v_{i},v_{j})$$

Where $v_{k}$ refers to neuron $k$'s activity 

Long-term potentiation can be observed through a simple experiment. First a neuron is given low-amplitude pulses so as to evoke a PSP but no action potential, and that PSP is measured. After a set amount of time, the frequency of pulses is increased significantly to invoke spikes. Later, the single pulse are used again to test the PSP of the neuron, which is found to be significantly higher than when tested in the intial phase of the experiment.
